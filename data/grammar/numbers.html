Numbers & telling time מִסְפָּרִים

Hebrew numbers come in a masculine and a feminine set, and the feminine set looks like it should be masculine. The practical shortcut: learn the feminine set first — it's what people use for counting, prices, phone numbers and the time.

Yes — the masculine forms end in ־ָה. It's backwards, and everyone finds it confusing.

Using them with nouns

  • The number comes before the noun: שָׁלוֹשׁ בִּירוֹת (3 beers, f.), שְׁלוֹשָׁה יָמִים (3 days, m.)
  • "One" comes after and acts like an adjective: בִּירָה אַחַת, יוֹם אֶחָד
  • "Two" shortens before a noun: שְׁתֵּי בִּירוֹת, שְׁנֵי חֲבֵרִים

Bigger numbers

11–19: unit + עֶשְׂרֵה (f.): אַחַת עֶשְׂרֵה, שְׁתֵּים עֶשְׂרֵה, חֲמֵשׁ עֶשְׂרֵה. Tens don't change: עֶשְׂרִים, שְׁלוֹשִׁים, אַרְבָּעִים, חֲמִישִּׁים, שִׁישִּׁים, שִׁבְעִים, שְׁמוֹנִים, תִּשְׁעִים. Combine with וְ: עֶשְׂרִים וְחָמֵשׁ (25). Hundreds: מֵאָה, מָאתַיִים, שְׁלוֹשׁ מֵאוֹת. Thousands: אֶלֶף, אַלְפַּיִים, שְׁלוֹשֶׁת אֲלָפִים.

On the street
  • Prices: עֶשְׂרִים וְחָמֵשׁ שֶׁקֶל — feminine number, singular שֶׁקֶל. Many people do this with all nouns (שָׁלוֹשׁ יָמִים) — common but marked as casual.
  • Phone numbers are read digit by digit, with אֶפֶס for zero: אֶפֶס חָמֵשׁ אַרְבַּע…
  • אַחַת־אַחַת — "one by one", and פִּי שְׁתַּיִים — "twice as much".

Morning / evening: שְׁמוֹנֶה בַּבּוֹקֶר, שְׁמוֹנֶה בָּעֶרֶב. Timetables use the 24-hour clock: עֶשְׂרִים וְאַחַת (21:00), but in conversation people say תֵּשַׁע בָּעֶרֶב.

Days of the week

Most days are just "day + ordinal number", starting from Sunday: יוֹם רִאשׁוֹן (Sun), שֵׁנִי (Mon), שְׁלִישִׁי (Tue), רְבִיעִי (Wed), חֲמִישִׁי (Thu), שִׁישִּׁי (Fri), שַׁבָּת (Sat). Sunday is a normal work day.
