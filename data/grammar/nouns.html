Nouns: gender & plural שֵׁם עֶצֶם

Every Hebrew noun is masculine or feminine, and that choice ripples through the sentence: adjectives, verbs and numbers all agree with it. The good news — you can usually tell from the ending.

Masculine or feminine?

  • Feminine nouns usually end in ־ָה (-a) or ־ת (-et, -it, -ut): דִּירָה, מִרְפֶּסֶת, מוֹנִית, חֲנוּת.
  • Masculine nouns are everything else: בַּיִת, שׁוּלְחָן, סֶרֶט, קַו.

When in doubt, listen for the adjective: עִיר גְּדוֹלָה tells you עִיר is feminine.

Plurals

Masculine plural: -im ־ִים. Feminine plural: -ot ־וֹת. The ending replaces the feminine ־ָה / ־ת.

SingularPlural
סֶרֶטסְרָטִיםmovies
חָבֵרחֲבֵרִיםfriends
דִּירָהדִּירוֹתapartments
מוֹנִיתמוֹנִיּוֹתtaxis
חֲנוּתחֲנוּיוֹתshops

The vowels inside often shift (סֶרֶט → סְרָטִים), and some nouns take the "wrong" ending: שֻׁלְחָן → שׁוּלְחָנוֹת, מָקוֹם → מְקוֹמוֹת, לַיְלָה → לֵילוֹת, אִישָּׁה → נָשִׁים (women), שָׁנָה → שָׁנִים (years). The gender stays the same — adjectives follow the noun's gender, not its ending: שָׁנִים טוֹבוֹת.

Pairs: ־ַיִים

Things that come in twos take -ayim: עֵינַיִים (eyes), נַעֲלַיִים (shoes), מִכְנָסַיִים (trousers), אוֹפַנַּיִים (bicycle). Also time: יוֹמַיִים (two days), שְׁבוּעַיִים (two weeks), פַּעֲמַיִים (twice).

The definite article: הַ־

"The" is a prefix: דִּירָה (an apartment) → הַדִּירָה (the apartment). There's no word for "a" — אֲנִי מְחַפֵּשׂ דִּירָה = I'm looking for an apartment. In speech, אֶחָד / אַחַת after the noun sometimes works like "a certain": בָּחוּר אֶחָד = some guy.

After ב, ל, כ the ה disappears into the prefix: בְּ + הַבַּיִת → בַּבַּיִת (in the house), לְ + הַיָּם → לַיָּם (to the sea). You hear it as "ba-", "la-".
