Questions & connecting sentences שְׁאֵלוֹת וּמִילּוֹת קִישּׁוּר

To go from single phrases to real conversation (A2), you need two things: asking questions, and joining clauses with because, when, if, that. Hebrew makes both fairly easy.

Yes/no questions

Just raise your voice at the end — no word order change: אַתָּה רָעֵב? (are you hungry?). In writing you'll occasionally see הַאִם at the start; in speech, never.

Prepositions go before the question word, never at the end: עַל מָה אַתֶּם מְדַבְּרִים? = what are you talking about?

Joining clauses

שֶׁ־ — that / which / who

One letter, attached to the next word, and you can't leave it out like English "that":

  • אֲנִי חוֹשֵׁב שֶׁזֶּה סָגוּר — I think (that) it's closed
  • הַבָּחוּר שֶׁפָּגַשְׁתִּי — the guy (that) I met
  • הוּא אָמַר שֶׁהוּא יָבוֹא — he said (that) he'll come — the tense stays as he said it!

מָתַי vs כְּשֶׁ־

"When" as a question is מָתַי?. "When" joining two statements is כְּשֶׁ־. Never מָתַי in the middle of a sentence like English "when I was young".

If-sentences

  • Real: אִם + future/present: אִם יִהְיֶה חַם, נֵלֵךְ לַיָּם — if it's hot, we'll go to the beach.
  • Unreal: אִילּוּ / אִם + הָיָה + present: אִם הָיָה לִי זְמַן, הָיִיתִי בָּא — if I had time, I'd come. (הָיִיתִי + present = "would")

Negation

לֹא before the verb: אֲנִי לֹא יוֹדֵעַ. With יֵשׁ it becomes אֵין: אֵין לִי זְמַן. "Never / nothing / nobody" still need לֹא: אַף פַּעַם לֹא, שׁוּם דָּבָר לֹא (or כְּלוּם), אַף אֶחָד לֹא בָּא. Double negatives are correct in Hebrew!
