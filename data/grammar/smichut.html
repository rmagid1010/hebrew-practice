Smichut: noun + noun סְמִיכוּת

English says "bus stop", "bedroom", "coffee cup". Hebrew does the same thing in reverse order — תַּחֲנַת אוֹטוֹבּוּס, "stop (of) bus" — and the first noun sometimes changes shape. This is called smichut ("leaning").

The basic rule

Main noun first, then the noun that describes it. No "of" in between:

HebrewLiterallyMeaning
חֲדַר שֵׁינָהroom (of) sleepbedroom
בֵּית קָפֶהhouse (of) coffeecafé
כּוֹס יַיִןglass (of) winea glass of wine
יוֹם הוּלֶּדֶתday (of) birthbirthday
שְׂכַר דִּירָהwage (of) apartmentrent
תַּחֲנַת אוֹטוֹבּוּסstop (of) busbus stop

The first noun changes

  • Feminine ־ָה becomes ־ַת: אֲרוּחָה → אֲרוּחַת בּוֹקֶר (breakfast), תַּחֲנָה → תַּחֲנַת רַכֶּבֶת
  • Masculine plural ־ִים becomes ־ֵי: שִׁיעוּרִים → שִׁיעוּרֵי בַּיִת (homework), מִשְׁקָפַיִים → מִשְׁקְפֵי שֶׁמֶשׁ
  • Some vowels shorten: בַּיִת → בֵּית, חֶדֶר → חֲדַר, שָׂכָר → שְׂכַר

Where does "the" go?

On the second noun only — and it makes the whole phrase definite:

  • בֵּית קָפֶה — a café → בֵּית הַקָּפֶה — the café
  • חֲדַר שֵׁינָה → חֲדַר הַשֵּׁינָה — the bedroom

An adjective goes at the very end and agrees with the first noun: בֵּית קָפֶה חָדָשׁ (a new café), אֲרוּחַת בּוֹקֶר טְעִימָה (a tasty breakfast).

On the street: just use שֶׁל

For ownership, speech prefers שֶׁל: הַדִּירָה שֶׁל דָּנִי (Dani's apartment), not דִּירַת דָּנִי. Smichut stays for fixed compound words — the ones above are all used daily. A rule of thumb: if English would make it one word or a fixed term (bedroom, bus stop, birthday), Hebrew uses smichut; if it's "X's Y", use שֶׁל.
