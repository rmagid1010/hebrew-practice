Gender & agreement זָכָר וּנְקֵבָה

In Hebrew, gender isn't a detail you can fix later. Every noun is masculine or feminine, and adjectives, present-tense verbs, “you” and commands all change with it. This site checks gender on every card.

1. Every noun comes with זֶה, זֹאת or אֵלֶּה

Noun cards don't ask for the bare word. The prompt says “this is a table” or “these are shoes”, and you answer with the matching word, so you learn the noun's gender together with the word:

HebrewUse it for
masc.זֶה שׁוּלְחָןa masculine noun
fem.זֹאת מִיטָּהa feminine noun
pluralאֵלֶּה כִּיסְאוֹת · אֵלֶּה נַעֲלַיִיםany plural, masculine or feminine

Type שולחן on its own, or זאת שולחן, and the card is marked wrong. The answer tells you the noun's gender. Plural nouns still have a gender: נַעֲלַיִים (shoes) is feminine, so they're נַעֲלַיִים חֲדָשׁוֹת.

In fast speech you'll hear זה used for everything (זה דירה יפה). It's understood, but it isn't correct, and this site always expects the matching form.

2. Who is speaking, and to whom?

“I'm tired” is אֲנִי עָיֵף for a man and אֲנִי עֲיֵפָה for a woman. “Can you…?” is אַתָּה יָכוֹל to a man and אַתְּ יְכוֹלָה to a woman. Sentences like these have several versions, and each review shows one of them with a badge:

  • ♂ You’re a man ♀ You’re a woman: who is talking (or writing).
  • ♂♂ a group: men or mixed ♀♀ a group of women: “we” and “they” versions. A group with even one man is masculine (אֲנַחְנוּ לוֹמְדִים); only all-women groups use the feminine plural (אֲנַחְנוּ לוֹמְדוֹת).
  • →♂ talking to a man →♀ talking to a woman →♂♀ talking to a group: who you're addressing.

Only the version the badge asks for counts. Cards rotate through their versions as you review them, so you practise every form. Writing tasks work the same way: the badge says who you are, and your verbs and adjectives have to match.

3. Adjectives and verbs follow the noun

Most course lessons now have an agreement drill: one card that rotates between a masculine, a feminine and a plural noun, so the adjective has to change each time.

What the checker looks at

When you type an answer, it checks four things:

  • זֶה / זֹאת / אֵלֶּה + noun, and הַ… הַזֶּה / הַזֹּאת / הָאֵלֶּה.
  • A noun + its adjective, or a subject noun + its present-tense verb: הַדִּירָה קְטַנָּה.
  • אֲנִי / אַתָּה / אַתְּ / הוּא / הִיא / הֵם… + verb or adjective, including your own gender after אֲנִי.
  • Commands and “you will…” forms against the person you're talking to.

Any agreement mistake makes the answer wrong, even when every word is otherwise spelled right. The checker only knows the course's words, so it can miss mistakes with others, and it doesn't check the past or future tense yet. If it ever flags something that's right, use the flag button.
