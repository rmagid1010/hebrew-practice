Reading Hebrew קְרִיאָה

Hebrew is written right to left with 22 letters, all consonants. Vowels are mostly not written — readers fill them in from knowing the words. This page covers what you need to read, and to type answers on this site.

How this site handles vowels

Prompts and answers are shown with vowel points (niqqud) so you always know how a word sounds. You never type them: answers are checked on the letters only. You can hide niqqud in Settings once you feel ready.

Sounds-alike letters (spelling traps)

Because modern pronunciation merged some sounds, these pairs sound identical — you just have to learn which one a word uses: ח / כ (ch), ט / ת (t), כּ / ק (k), שׂ / ס (s), ב / ו (v), א / ע (silent).

The dot inside a letter (dagesh)

In ב כ פ the dot changes the sound: בּ=b / ב=v, כּ=k / כ=ch, פּ=p / פ=f. At the start of a word they're almost always b/k/p. That's why the same root can sound different: כּוֹתֵב (kotev) but אֶכְתּוֹב (echtov).

Full spelling (what Israelis actually write)

Without niqqud, modern Hebrew adds the letters ו and י as vowel hints. This is called full spelling (כְּתִיב מָלֵא), and it's what you type here.

  • ו marks o or u: שֻׁלְחָן → שולחן (table)
  • י marks i: סִפֵּר → סיפר (told), דִּבֵּר → דיבר
  • A consonant v in the middle of a word is doubled: שווה (shave, worth it)
  • A consonant y in the middle of a word is doubled: עניינים (inyanim)
The answer checker is forgiving about spelling, strict about letters

If you leave out a vowel letter (שלחן for שולחן) or use a regular letter instead of a final one (שולחנ), you'll get Almost and the card will be marked Hard. You can always override the grade. In verb drills, it's strict: one letter can change the person or tense.
