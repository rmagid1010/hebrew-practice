Nif'al בִּנְיַן נִפְעַל

Nif'al is mostly the passive or "it happens to you" partner of pa'al — but plenty of everyday verbs live here with an active meaning: enter, stay, meet up, be careful, fall asleep.

The shape

With the root כ־נ־ס (enter):

  • Present: ni–a → נִכְנָס (nichnas)
  • Past: ni–a → נִכְנַס (nichnas) — the same sound as the present for "he"!
  • Future: yi–a–e with a doubled first letter → יִיכָּנֵס (yikanes)
  • Infinitive: lehi–a–e → לְהִיכָּנֵס (lehikanes)

The tell-tale sign: a נ before the root in the past and present, and הִי־ in the infinitive.

Everyday nif'al

נִגְמַר לִי הַכֶּסֶף = I ran out of money (lit. the money finished to me) · הַחֲנוּת נִסְגְּרָה = the shop closed · נִרְאֶה = we'll see · נִדְמֶה לִי = it seems to me · נִשְׁבַּר לִי = I've had it (lit. it broke for me).

Present: נִכְנָס · נִכְנֶסֶת · נִכְנָסִים · נִכְנָסוֹת. Command: הִיכָּנֵס! — but people say תִּיכָּנֵס! ("come in!").

Watch the sounds

In the past and present the first root letter follows a closed syllable, so ב כ פ are soft: נִפְגַּשׁ (nifgash). In the future and infinitive that letter is doubled, so it's hard: נִיפָּגֵשׁ (nipagesh). Same verb, f ↔ p.

If the first root letter is א ה ח ע ר it can't double, and the vowel before it lengthens: לְהֵירָדֵם (leheradem, to fall asleep), אֵירָדֵם.

Spelling

Modern spelling adds י in the future and infinitive: ייכנס, תיכנס, להיכנס. Many people skip it in texts (תכנס) — the drills accept both.
