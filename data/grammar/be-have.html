"To be" and "to have" in past & future הָיָה, יִהְיֶה, הָיָה לִי

In the present, Hebrew skips "to be" (אֲנִי עָיֵיף = I'm tired) and says "to have" with יֵשׁ לִי. But in the past and future you need לִהְיוֹת — and with "having", it agrees with the thing, not the person.

לִהְיוֹת — to be

  • יִהְיֶה בְּסֵדֶר — it'll be fine

Had / will have: הָיָה לִי

יֵשׁ לִי X is literally "there is X to me". So in the past and future, the verb agrees with X:

On the street

Many people just use הָיָה for everything — הָיָה לִי בְּעָיָה, הָיָה לִי שְׁנֵי חֲבֵרִים. You'll hear it constantly; the agreeing form is what you'd write.

Same trick with other "to me" phrases

  • קַר לִי → הָיָה לִי קַר (I was cold)
  • בָּא לִי → בָּא לִי works for the past too in speech, or הִתְחַשֵּׁק לִי
  • קָשֶׁה לִי → הָיָה לִי קָשֶׁה (it was hard for me)
  • צָרִיךְ → הָיָה צָרִיךְ (it was necessary / someone should have)

"Would" and "used to"

הָיָה + present tense makes "would" or "used to":

  • הָיִיתִי בָּא, אֲבָל אֵין לִי זְמַן — I'd come, but I don't have time
  • כְּשֶׁהָיִיתִי יֶלֶד, הָיִינוּ הוֹלְכִים לַיָּם כָּל שַׁבָּת — when I was a kid, we used to go to the beach every Saturday
  • הָיִיתִי רוֹצֶה… — I would like… (polite)
