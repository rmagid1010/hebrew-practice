Must, can, allowed: modal words צָרִיךְ, יָכוֹל, אֶפְשָׁר

"I need to…", "can I…?", "you're not allowed to…" — these words + an infinitive get you through most of daily life. Some agree with the person, some never change.

Past and future use הָיָה / יִהְיֶה: הָיִיתִי צָרִיךְ (I had to / should have), אֲנִי אֶצְטָרֵךְ (I'll need), יָכוֹלְתִּי (I could — its own past), אוּכַל (I'll be able; spoken: אֲנִי אוּכַל).

To say who with the impersonal words, add לְ: אָסוּר לְךָ לְעַשֵּׁן (you're not allowed to smoke), כְּדַאי לָךְ לָבוֹא (you should come, to a woman).

On the street
  • אֶפְשָׁר? on its own = "may I?" — pointing at a chair, a lighter, a fry.
  • אֵין מָה לַעֲשׂוֹת = nothing to be done / that's life.
  • חַיָּיב! = you have to (see / try / go there) — the ultimate recommendation.
  • כְּדַאי לְךָ can be friendly advice or a threat, depending on the tone 😄
