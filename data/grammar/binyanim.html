Roots & binyanim שׁוֹרָשִׁים וּבִנְיָנִים

Almost every Hebrew verb is built from two pieces: a root of (usually) three consonants that carries the core meaning, and a binyan — a verb pattern that adds vowels, prefixes and sometimes a doubled letter. Once you see this, new verbs stop looking random.

The root (שׁוֹרֶשׁ)

Take the root כ־ת־ב, which has to do with writing. Pour it into different patterns and you get a whole family:

WordMeaning
כָּתַבkatavhe wrote
מִכְתָּבmichtava letter
כְּתוֹבֶתktovetan address
כַּתָּבkatava reporter
הִתְכַּתֵּבhitkatevhe corresponded (wrote back and forth)

Dictionaries and teachers name roots with dashes or dots: כ־ת־ב or כ.ת.ב. The letters ו and י in a word are often just vowel helpers, not part of the root.

The seven binyanim

Each binyan is named after how the dummy root פ־ע־ל ("do") looks in it. The table gives one real verb in each, in past (he), present (m. sing.), future (he) and infinitive.

Spotting the binyan from the infinitive

The infinitive is the best clue, which is why the Core Verbs deck teaches infinitives. Each pattern is shown with the dummy root פ־ע־ל — swap in the real root letters:

  • לִפְעוֹל → pa'al: לִכְתּוֹב, לִגְמוֹר, לִסְגּוֹר
  • לְהִיפָּעֵל → nif'al: לְהִיכָּנֵס, לְהִיפָּגֵשׁ
  • לְפַעֵל → pi'el: לְדַבֵּר, לְשַׁלֵּם, לְחַפֵּשׂ
  • לְהַפְעִיל → hif'il: לְהַזְמִין, לְהַתְחִיל, לְהַרְגִּישׁ
  • לְהִתְפַּעֵל → hitpa'el: לְהִתְלַבֵּשׁ, לְהִתְקַשֵּׁר

Pu'al and huf'al are passive, so they have no infinitive.

One root, several binyanim

This is where binyanim start paying off — you can guess meanings:

ס־ד־רלְסַדֵּרpi'elto arrange, to sort out

כ־נ־סלְהִיכָּנֵסnif'alto enter, to go in
לְהַכְנִיסhif'ilto put in, to let in

(In לְהִסְתַּדֵּר the ת of hitpa'el swaps places with the ס of the root. It does that with ס, שׁ, שׂ, צ and ז.)

Tenses work the same way in every binyan

Hebrew has three tenses. The binyan changes the vowels inside, but the person markers are shared:

  • Present works like an adjective: 4 forms — m. singular, f. singular, m. plural, f. plural. You need the pronoun: אֲנִי כּוֹתֵב, הִיא כּוֹתֶבֶת.
  • Past adds endings — so the pronoun is optional: כָּתַבְתִּי already means "I wrote".
  • Future adds prefixes (and some endings).
PersonPast endingFuture prefix/ending
I אֲנִי־תִּי-tiאֶ־e-
you m. אַתָּה־תָּ-taתִּ־ti-
you f. אַתְּ־תְּ-tתִּ־ … ־יti- … -i
he הוּא— (the base form)יִ־yi-
she הִיא־ה-aתִּ־ti-
we אֲנַחְנוּ־נוּ-nuנִ־ni-
you pl. אַתֶּם־תֶּם-temתִּ־ … ־וּti- … -u
they הֵם־וּ-uיִ־ … ־וּyi- … -u

Notice: in the future, "you (m.)" and "she" are the same form (תִּכְתּוֹב). Context tells them apart.

On the street

The feminine plurals אַתֶּן / הֵן have their own textbook forms, but most people just use the masculine plural forms for everyone. The drills on this site practice the forms you'll actually hear.

Weak roots

Some roots contain letters that can't do what the pattern asks — ו or י in the middle (גָּר, בָּא), ה at the end (רוֹצֶה, קוֹנֶה), נ or י at the start (נוֹסֵעַ, יוֹשֵׁב), or a throaty letter (א ה ח ע ר) that changes the vowels nearby. They follow their own mini-patterns. Many of the most common verbs are weak, so you'll learn them as words first — the pa'al page has a table of the ones you use daily.

How to learn verbs

  1. Learn each new verb as infinitive + binyan (the Core Verbs notes show both).
  2. Learn the present tense first — it's what you'll use most.
  3. Then practice past (אָכַלְתִּי, הָלַכְתִּי, דִּיבַּרְתִּי) and future one binyan at a time with the drills.
