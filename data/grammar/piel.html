Pi'el בִּנְיַן פִּיעֵל

Pi'el is the binyan with a "doubled" middle root letter (marked with a dot, the dagesh). It holds tons of everyday verbs — speak, pay, look for, wait, try, travel around — and it's where Hebrew puts most new and slang verbs.

The shape

With the root ד־ב־ר:

  • Present: me–a–e → מְדַבֵּר (medaber)
  • Past: i–e → דִּיבֵּר (diber)
  • Future: ye–a–e → יְדַבֵּר (yedaber)
  • Infinitive: le–a–e → לְדַבֵּר (ledaber)

Two things to notice:

  1. The present tense always starts with מְ — that's your clue it's not pa'al.
  2. In the past, modern spelling adds a י after the first letter: דיבר, שילם, חיפש, סידר. Present and future don't have it.

Because the middle letter is "doubled", ב כ פ in the middle are always hard: לְסַפֵּר (lesaPer, to tell) vs. pa'al סוֹפֵר (soFer, counts).

As in every binyan, everyday commands use the future: תְּשַׁלֵּם בַּקּוּפָּה = pay at the register; אַל תְּחַכֶּה לִי = don't wait for me.

The last two have roots ending in ה, so they end in -e / -a instead of a consonant — like רוֹצֶה in pa'al.

When the middle letter is ר, א or ע

These letters can't be doubled, so the first vowel stretches instead: לְסָרֵב (lesarev, to refuse) → past סֵירֵב (serev); לְתָאֵר (leta'er, to describe) → תֵּיאֵר (te'er). Same binyan, slightly different vowels.

Pi'el is the slang factory

When Hebrew borrows or invents a verb, it usually lands in pi'el — four-letter roots fit right in:

  • לְסַמֵּס (lesames) — to text (from SMS): תְּסַמֵּס לִי = text me
  • לְגַגֵּל (legagel) — to google
  • לְפַרְגֵּן (lefargen) — to be genuinely happy for someone, to give credit (from Yiddish)
  • לְבַלְבֵּל (levalbel) — to confuse; אַל תְּבַלְבֵּל אֶת הַמּוֹחַ = stop messing with my head
  • לְטַלְפֵּן (letalfen) — to phone

Pi'el and pu'al

Pu'al is pi'el's passive. You'll rarely need its verbs, but its present tense gives very common adjectives:

  • סִידֵּר arranged → מְסוּדָּר tidy, organized, sorted
  • בִּישֵּׁל cooked → מְבוּשָּׁל cooked
  • בִּלְבֵּל confused → מְבוּלְבָּל confused
  • עִנְיֵין interested → מְעוּנְיָין interested
