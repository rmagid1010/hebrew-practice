Adjectives, "this" & comparisons שֵׁם תּוֹאַר

Adjectives come after the noun and agree with it in gender, number and definiteness. Once you've got that, "this", "more", "the most" and "too" are easy.

Four forms

MasculineFeminine
Singularגָּדוֹלגְּדוֹלָה
Pluralגְּדוֹלִיםגְּדוֹלוֹת

Endings: f. ־ָה (or ־ת), m. pl. ־ִים, f. pl. ־וֹת. Adjectives ending in ־ִי take ־ִית: טִבְעוֹנִי → טִבְעוֹנִית.

Word order and "the"

  • דִּירָה גְּדוֹלָה — a big apartment (noun first!)
  • הַדִּירָה הַגְּדוֹלָה — the big apartment: the adjective gets הַ too
  • הַדִּירָה גְּדוֹלָה — the apartment is big. No הַ on the adjective = a sentence. Hebrew has no "is" in the present.
One little ה changes everything

הַקָּפֶה הַטָּעִים = the tasty coffee. הַקָּפֶה טָעִים = the coffee is tasty.

This / that

m.f.pl.
this / theseזֶהזֹאת (זוֹ)אֵלֶּה
  • זֶה טוֹב — this is good (as a sentence)
  • הַסֶּרֶט הַזֶּה — this movie (after the noun, with הַ on both)
  • הַסִּדְרָה הַזֹּאת — this series

In speech זֶה is used for almost anything: מָה זֶה?, זֶה לֹא בְּסֵדֶר.

Unlike English, there are no special forms like "better" or "biggest" — just יוֹתֵר טוֹב (better), הֲכִי טוֹב (best).

On the street: intensifiers

מַמָּשׁ טוֹב (really good), מָלֵא אֲנָשִׁים (full of people), סוּפֶּר נֶחְמָד (super nice), רְצַח (killer — חַם רְצַח = murderously hot), … אֶחָד גָּדוֹל (one big… — בָּלָגָן אֶחָד גָּדוֹל = one big mess), and the all-purpose אֵיזֶה…!: אֵיזֶה יוֹפִי!
