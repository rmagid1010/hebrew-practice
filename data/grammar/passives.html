Pu'al & huf'al פּוּעַל וְהוּפְעַל

These are the two purely passive binyanim: pu'al is the passive of pi'el, huf'al the passive of hif'il. You won't use them much as verbs in conversation — but their present tense gives you dozens of everyday adjectives.

Spot them by the "u"

Both have an u sound after the first letter / prefix, and no infinitive.

Pu'al (passive of pi'el)Huf'al (passive of hif'il)
Active verbסִידֵּרarrangedהִזְמִיןinvited
Pastסוּדַּרsudar — was arrangedהוּזְמַןhuzman — was invited
Presentמְסוּדָּרmesudar — arranged / tidyמוּזְמָןmuzman — invited
Futureיְסוּדַּרyesudarיוּזְמַןyuzman

The present works like an adjective with four forms: מְסוּדָּר · מְסוּדֶּרֶת · מְסוּדָּרִים · מְסוּדָּרוֹת.

On the street

אַתָּה מוּזְמָן! = you're welcome to (come / take some). מָה מוּמְלָץ? = what do you recommend? אַתָּה מוּכָן? = are you ready? הַכּוֹל מְסוּדָּר = it's all sorted.

How people avoid the passive

In speech, Hebrew usually prefers a subject-less plural ("they") over a passive verb:

  • פִּיטְּרוּ אוֹתוֹ (lit. "they fired him") rather than הוּא פּוּטַּר — he got fired
  • גָּנְבוּ לִי אֶת הָאוֹפַנַּיִים — my bike got stolen (lit. "they stole me the bike")
  • אוֹמְרִים שֶׁ… — they say that… / it's said that…

So: recognize pu'al and huf'al, use their adjectives, and use "they" for the rest.
